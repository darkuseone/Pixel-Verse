# «Intaglio Pop» — design philosophy of the «Land of the Fee» cast

**The movement.** Intaglio Pop takes the most serious picture a country owns — the engraved portrait on its money — and lets cartoon idiots live inside it. Every figure is drawn as if a bank-note engraver, a master of the burin with forty years at the plate, had been ordered to portray a broke eagle screaming into a phone. The seriousness of the craft is the joke: painstaking, meticulously crafted line work in service of the pettiest disasters of everyday life.

**Form and line.** Shadows are not dithered, not frosted, not cross-hatched at random: they are cut. Each shadow tone is a field of parallel diagonal engraved lines on a fixed plate grid (they never boil between frames), the deeper shadow adds a second, crossing set, exactly as on a bill. Highlights are bare cream paper. The outline is banknote ink — a deep money green, one sprite pixel, never black. Silhouettes are monumental and simple (a barrel with a hook, a pear on matchsticks, a perm on a cardigan, a circle with a smile) so that the dense engraving lives inside a shape that reads in one second at thumbnail size. Every engraved line must look placed by hand with master-level patience.

**Color and material.** Paper cream, money green, seal red, a tired navy, gold leaf for anything precious (the medallion, the coin, the price). Each hero owns one accent that no other hero touches: Duke the gold of a seal, Sal the oil-slick violet-green of a city neck, Fee-Bee a synthetic mint that glows instead of being lit, Doris the peach of a church-basement cardigan. The world behind them is sunny satire in the same red-white-blue and mint register, so heroes and backgrounds sit on one printed sheet.

**Faces.** Detailed and alive at 2× resolution and up: eyes with whites, irises, pupils and a glint; lids that drop for deadpan; brows that carry the whole character (Duke's are permanently furrowed — an eagle cannot look anything but disappointed, even when happy). The beak is the mouth: it opens with a visible tongue; feathers bristle when he roars. Sal's tiny head bobs on its own rhythm. Fee-Bee has no body at all, only a face of light on whatever screen she licenses this week, with an equalizer that dances to her words.

**Scale and rhythm.** Proportions are deliberately absurd and consistent: Duke's chest is wider than a doorway, his feet are tiny; Sal is the size of Duke's head; Doris is half Duke's height and twice his calm. Contrast drives the comedy — the grand engraved hero against the small receipt that defeats him. Every sheet, every frame should feel like the product of deep expertise and countless hours: crisp pixels, no mush, no smooth AI gradients, nothing accidental.

**Difference from every other cast of the channel.** Not the plum ink and frost of «Thanks, Randy», not the tabloid halftone of «Florida Man», not the snow-lubok of «Прохор», not the dithered capsule rigs of «Agent Dibs», «Sunny Palms» or «Grim Ride»: here the shadow is an engraved line, the outline is money ink, and the main hero is a bird.
